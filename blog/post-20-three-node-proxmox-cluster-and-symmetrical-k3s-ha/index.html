The Third Physical NUC: Retiring the NAS Quorum for True 3-Node Physical HA

16 August 2026

Tags: homelab, proxmox, kubernetes, k3s, ha, etcd, corosync, opentofu, ansible, hardware

Odd-numbered cluster topologies are the golden rule of distributed consensus. When you only have two physical hypervisors, you end up relying on virtualized witnesses, QDevices on network attached storage, or tainted quorum VMs. But when a third physical node arrives, everything changes. In this post, I cover how adding a third Intel NUC enabled us to retire the Synology VMM quorum setup, form a native 3-node Proxmox cluster, and deploy a symmetrical 6-node HA Kubernetes topology completely contained within VLAN 99.

The Setup

Historically, the homelab operated with two physical compute nodes (pve01 and pve02). To maintain high availability and prevent split-brain:

  • Proxmox Quorum: Handled by a Debian QDevice VM (corosync-qnetd) running on the Synology DS920+ NAS in Management VLAN 1 (192.168.10.13).
  • k3s etcd Consensus: Maintained with a third control-plane VM (k3s-server-03, 192.168.10.23) also running in Synology Virtual Machine Manager (VMM), tainted with dedicated=control-plane:NoSchedule so it didn't consume NAS resources running pods.

While this worked reliably (and taught us a lot about distributed quorum), it introduced cross-VLAN dependencies, split failure domains across compute and storage hardware, and consumed memory on the NAS that was better suited for storage and backups.

When a third physical Intel NUC 7 i7 (NUC7i7BNH / CPU i7-7567U, 16GB RAM, 256GB NVMe) joined the rack, we had the hardware needed for a clean architectural redesign.

What I Built

1. Native 3-Node Proxmox VE Cluster

With the 3rd physical host installed on Homelab VLAN 99 as pve03 (192.168.99.13), Corosync now runs directly across the three physical NUCs:

  • pve01 (24GB RAM, 1 vote)
  • pve02 (24GB RAM, 1 vote)
  • pve03 (16GB RAM, 1 vote)

We decommissioned the Synology QDevice (pvecm qdevice remove), moving to a native 3-vote Corosync ring with full 1-node fault tolerance.

2. Symmetrical 6-Node k3s HA Topology

Instead of running uneven or tainted VMs, we established a symmetrical design where every physical Proxmox host runs exactly 1 control-plane VM and 1 worker VM:

  • k3s-server-03 is now an untainted, full control-plane peer participating in etcd consensus and workload scheduling alongside k3s-server-01 and k3s-server-02.
  • k3s-agent-03 provides dedicated worker capacity on the 3rd host.
  • All 6 VMs reside entirely on VLAN 99 (192.168.99.0/24).

3. Codified in IaC and Ansible

  • OpenTofu (infra/): Added proxmox_node_tertiary and VM resources for k3s_server_03 and k3s_agent_03.
  • Ansible (config/): Updated inventory/hosts.yaml to purge all qdevice groups, and standardized k3s-server-03.yaml and os-patching-proxmox.yaml for serial 3-node lifecycle management.

The Decision

This architectural shift is documented in ADR-021: 3-Node Physical Proxmox VE Cluster & Symmetrical k3s HA Topology, which supersedes the historical 2-node + QDevice decision in ADR-002.

Why Symmetrical Hypervisor / VM Pairing?

  1. Predictable Failure Domains: If any physical NUC fails or reboots for maintenance, the cluster loses exactly 1/3 of its control-plane quorum (leaving 2/3 votes, fully operational) and 1/3 of worker scheduling capacity.
  2. Eliminated Cross-VLAN Witnessing: All Corosync and k3s traffic remains isolated inside VLAN 99. The Synology NAS on VLAN 1 is purely a high-throughput NFS storage target.

What This Taught Me

  • Witnesses Are Great For Scarcity, Physical Quorum Is Better For Simplicity: Using Synology VMM for QDevice and tainted control plane VMs was a great way to build HA on a budget, but removing that layer reduces operational complexity and inter-device dependencies significantly.
  • Documentation & IaC First: Updating the Single Source of Truth (environment-data.md), OpenTofu definitions, Ansible playbooks, and execution procedures before touching live infrastructure makes large-scale platform migrations smooth and error-free.
